📚 Onshape lessons

Onshape Intro to CAD — Unit 2: Designing a Skateboard. Use lessons 2.1–2.4. Each lesson has a slide deck and a student guide.

Watch first: parts, revolve, and mates

Read the transcript

For two weeks, you'll model a skateboard in Onshape. The skills you learn here build your fingerboard and your skate park later. A skateboard is a set of parts that fit together. The deck. The trucks: a baseplate, a hanger, and a bushing. And the wheels, each with a bearing. To make round parts, you use revolve. Draw a flat profile next to an axis, then spin it all the way around. That's how you make wheels and bearings. Later, it's how you could make a ramp or a bowl. In the Assembly, mates connect the parts. A wheel mate leaves one degree of freedom: it can spin, but it can't fall off. The deck is fixed, so it doesn't move at all. Pay close attention to the truck bolt holes. Their spacing decides whether the trucks fit. This week the board is full size. In Week 9, you'll measure real fingerboard trucks and place holes that actually fit.

Week 6 — model the deck and trucks

  • 2.1 Designing the Deck. Fully constrain a deck sketch, extrude, fillet, and add the truck bolt-hole pattern.
  • 2.2 Designing the Trucks. Build the three truck parts (baseplate, hanger, bushing) with materials and mass.

Screencast: build the trucks (Lesson 2.2)

Stuck on 2.2? Watch it once, then pause and follow along in your own document.

Read the transcript

This is the Lesson 2.1 deck. Now we add a truck: baseplate, hanger and axle, bushing, and kingpin. Right-click Part 1 → Rename → Deck. Bottom view. Start a sketch on the bottom face of the deck. Show Sketch 2 (the bolt holes) with its eye icon. Offset tool: pick the 4 lines of the bolt-hole rectangle, arrow outward, 0.25 in. The bigger rectangle is the baseplate outline. Extrude: New, 0.25 in, away from the deck. A new grey plate under the deck. Sketch on the Front plane. Use tool: bring in the plate's bottom edge. Corner rectangle under the plate, from the end of that edge. A second, smaller rectangle below it, lined up on the right. Dimensions: 0.5 and 0.375 tall, 1.5 wide for the lower block. Extrude: Add to the plate, 0.75 in, Symmetric. Rename it Truck Baseplate. Hanger: sketch on the Front plane. Use the notch edge as a reference. Two circles, 0.75 below the notch: Ø0.25 (axle) and Ø0.75 (hanger). Black = fully defined. Small circle: New, 8.5 in, Symmetric. That's the axle. Ring: Add to the axle part, 6 in, Symmetric. That's the hanger. Rename it Truck Hanger. Hide the deck to see better. Bushing: sketch on the baseplate's big bottom face. Dotted lines find the center. Two circles on the center: Ø1 and Ø0.375. Extrude both regions: New, 0.25 in, away from the baseplate. Rename it Bushing. It sits right under the baseplate. Kingpin: hide the bushing, show Sketch 6, extrude the small circle: Add to Truck Baseplate, 0.75 in. The kingpin runs through the bushing into the hanger. Fillet (Shift+F): baseplate edges, 0.125 in. Rounded edges look better and are safer to hold. Right-click the hanger → Edit appearance → light grey. Assign material: Aluminum - 356 for the baseplate and the hanger. Bushing: any color, material Polyurethane. Mass properties: select the 3 truck parts. Ours ≈ 0.55 lb. Board from below with one truck. You'll copy it in Lesson 2.4.

Week 7 — wheels, bearings, and assembly

  • 2.3 Wheels and Bearings. Use revolve to make round parts, with construction lines and diameter dimensions.
  • 2.4 Putting It All Together. In the Assembly tab, fix the deck, make the wheels spin, insert hardware, and use mate connectors and degrees of freedom.

Screencast: wheels and bearings (Lesson 2.3)

Revolve is the new tool here. Watch how the profile sits above the centerline before you spin it.

Read the transcript

Click + at the bottom left → Create Part Studio. Right-click the tab → Rename → Wheel and Bearing. Sketch on the Top plane. Press N. Line from the origin to the right. Press Q: construction. It's the axle line. Two center-point rectangles above the line: wide (tire) on top, narrow (hub) below. Top rectangle: 36 mm wide. Type mm after the number. Bottom rectangle: 0.5 in wide. Top line + centerline, drag below the centerline → a diameter. Ø54 mm: the outside of the wheel. Middle line: Ø22 mm. Hole: Ø0.25 in. If it flips below the line, dimension from the middle line instead. Revolve: both rectangles, axis = construction line, Full. Rename the part Wheel. P hides the planes. Fillet the outer edges. Now it looks like a real wheel. Side view. The flat ring around the hole is where the bearing sits. Extrude that face: New, 7 mm. Rename it Bearing. Sketch on the bearing face: two circles on the center. 1 mm in from each edge: Ø20 mm and Ø8.35 mm. Ring: Remove, 0.5 mm. That's the bearing groove. Materials: Bearing = Stainless Steel 316. Wheel = Polyurethane. Edit appearance: pick a wheel color. Mass properties: ≈ 0.23 lb for one wheel and bearing. One wheel and bearing. Lesson 2.4 puts four on the board.

Screencast: put it all together (Lesson 2.4)

This video uses two shortcuts: Fix for the truck parts, and Mirror for the second truck. The lesson guide does the same thing with fastened mates and copy-paste. Either way works.

Read the transcript

Open the Assembly tab. Parts come together here and can move. Click Insert. Both Part Studios are listed. Click Skateboard Deck and Trucks, then the green check. Don't click the window. Right-click the deck → Fix. Select the 3 truck parts → Fix. (The lesson text uses fastened mates.) Second truck: in the Part Studio, open Mirror. Pick the 3 truck parts, Right plane, New (not Add). In the assembly, insert the 3 new parts, then Fix them. Insert Wheel and Bearing. It lands in the middle. Select wheel + bearing → Group. Hide the deck to see the axles. Revolute mate: hover the hanger end until the connector appears, click. Click the bearing face. The wheel jumps onto the axle. Insert Wheel and Bearing 3 more times. Widen the panel. Wheel 2 goes with Bearing 2, and so on. Group each pair: 2, 3, 4. Revolute the next wheel to the other truck. Shift+7, then the arrow keys to turn the view. Wheel over the hanger? It's backwards. Click Flip primary axis. Same for the last wheel. Show the deck. Two trucks, four wheels. Drag a wheel: it spins. Your complete skateboard. Challenge: add bolts and nuts from Standard content.

Try it: take the board apart

A skateboard is a set of separate parts. Pull it apart to see each one, then spin the wheels: a wheel mate leaves exactly one degree of freedom.

One thing you add

  • Bolt-hole connection note (after 2.1). In your paper notebook, sketch the truck-mounting layout and write one sentence on why hole placement matters for fit. Those holes are the exact Week 9 problem: you’ll caliper the real fingerboard trucks and place mounting holes that actually fit.

Looking ahead: the mates and degrees of freedom from 2.4 are the same skill the 12 capstone groups use to snap park sections to one connection standard; and the revolve from 2.3 is how you’d make a ramp or bowl. Early finishers can start a “Skate Ramp” Part Studio — a real head start on capstone design.

Heads-up on scale: Unit 2 models a full-size skateboard. Your Week 9 fingerboard deck is modeled at real fingerboard scale (96 mm), measured with calipers — not a shrunk-down copy of this board. You’ll re-apply the same skills at actual fingerboard dimensions.
